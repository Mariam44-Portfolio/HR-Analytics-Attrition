# HR Attrition Dashboard | Tableau

HR Attrition Dashboard in Tableau - 237 employees left out of 1,470 (16.12% attrition rate), Avg Monthly Income $6,503.

## 📊 Project Overview
Analysed HR data for 1,470 employees to understand why employees leave and which groups are most at risk. Built an interactive dashboard in Tableau Desktop Public Edition.

## 🎯 Key Metrics
- **Total Employees:** 1,470
- **Attrition Count:** 237
- **Attrition Rate:** 16.12%
- **Employees Stayed:** 1,233 (83.88%)
- **Avg Monthly Income:** $6,503

## 🔍 Key Findings

**1. 1 in 6 employees left**
237 out of 1,470 employees left the company - an attrition rate of 16.12%.

**2. Research & Development is the biggest department - and highest attrition in numbers**
R&D has ~960 employees, followed by Sales ~450, and Human Resources ~60. Most leavers came from R&D and Sales.

**3. Low income = Higher attrition**
Employees in the 0K - 3K monthly income band had the highest attrition. As income rises, attrition falls. Pay is a key retention factor.

**4. Overtime drives attrition**
Employees who work overtime (Yes) leave at a much higher rate than those who don't (No). Overtime is a strong warning sign.

**5. Low job satisfaction = Higher risk**
Employees with Job Satisfaction level 1 and 2 left more than those with level 3 and 4. Satisfaction protects retention.

**6. Young employees leave more**
Age Group 25-35 has the highest count of employees and the highest number of leavers. Early-career retention needs attention.

## 💡 Recommendations
1. Review pay for low-income bands (0K-3K) - link pay to performance and market rate
2. Reduce overtime - hire or redistribute workload, especially in R&D and Sales
3. Improve job satisfaction - manager check-ins, career growth, recognition for levels 1-2
4. Focus retention on 25-35 age group - mentorship, clear promotion path
5. Track attrition monthly - flag teams with overtime + low satisfaction early
